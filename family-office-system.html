In plain English

What actually happens

You don't need to be technical. Think of it as texting a group chat of experts who already have your files in front of them.

01

You ask

Type a command like /cio ("run my investment committee") or /tax ("find me savings") — or drop a statement in a folder and say /sync.

02

The team works

The right specialist quietly spins up a team of researchers who look things up at the same time — all grounded in your goals and holdings.

03

You get an answer

A clear written report with a plain recommendation — and the why. Stuck on jargon? /eli5 re-explains it like you're five.

04

It remembers

The decision and the reasoning get saved. Next time, every specialist already knows what happened — you never start from scratch.

The point: a regular chatbot forgets you the moment the window closes, and answers each question in isolation. This is a standing team with a shared, growing memory of your money — which is exactly what makes a real family office valuable.
The big idea

How it compounds & connects

Two ideas make this more than a pile of separate tools. Connect: every specialist reads the same picture of your finances, so your tax agent already knows what your investment chief is doing. Compound: every answer is saved back into that picture, so the system gets sharper and more personalized each time.

Under the hood

How a "team" skill runs

The heavyweight skills don't answer in a single pass. One command puts several specialist researchers on the problem at once, then a lead synthesizes their findings into a single coherent report — fast, deep, and consistent.

You run /cio
↓
The lead reads your profile
goals · investment policy · holdings · risk limits
↓  fans out to a team  ↓
Macro Equities Technicals Options Risk Alternatives
↓  findings merge  ↓
One synthesized report
a single voice, no contradictions — saved & dated
Why it works: parallelism gives speed (a whole team researching at once), specialization gives depth (each agent focuses narrowly), and the synthesizing lead gives coherence (one report, one voice). The shared profile keeps everyone grounded in the same facts.

The paper trail

How it saves its work

Every analysis is written to a dated markdown file under a category folder, so your history is sortable, searchable, and yours forever. Investment calls always use the same structure — so the reasoning, risk, and exit are never left implicit.

Every report opens the same way

# [Report Title]
**Date:** [Today's date]
**Agent:** [Specialist role]
**Prepared for:** [Your household]
---

Every recommendation is structured

## Recommendation
Action:        BUY / SELL / HOLD / TRIM
Conviction:    HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW
Position Size: X% of portfolio
Risk:          [what could go wrong]
Time Horizon:  [when it plays out]
Invalidation:  [what changes the call]
Built-in second opinion. Add --challenge to any investment skill and it appends a Devil's Advocate section that argues the opposite case with full conviction — a guard against your own confirmation bias.

What matters more than the specific APIs: the discipline of prioritizing structured data over web search, telling each researcher the priority order explicitly, and defining a clean fallback for every data type. Without that, an AI defaults to web search and quality collapses.
